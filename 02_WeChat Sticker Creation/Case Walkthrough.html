Just three steps:
Create a character → write a story → make a GIF

No skill packs, coding, or account sign-ups needed. Follow these three steps in a conversation:

Step OneCreate the Main Character
Define the character in one reusable description (species, art style, and colors). Paste the exact same description into every later prompt to keep the look consistent.
Step TwoWrite a Four-Panel Story
Setup → trigger → punchline → loop. The last panel should connect back to the first so the animation loops smoothly.
Step ThreeMake the GIF
Send the four images from step two to the AI and ask it to assemble them into a looping GIF with code. Then share it on WeChat.

Step One:
Create the main character first.

The character is the heart of a sticker pack:the mascot. Design it first and use it for every sticker to keep the look consistent. The key is to make the character description easy to reuse:write it as one short block—then paste the same description into each later prompt.

Create an original character sheet for a WeChat sticker. [Character] An adorable overworked office-worker mascot in a storybook illustration style, with a rounded body and a head-to-body ratio of about 1:1. Give it a pair of small light-brown cow horns, upright white horse ears in front of the horns, a fluffy brown mane flowing from the back of its head, a slightly elongated round face with a rounded muzzle, tiny black eyes, rosy cheeks, and a tired, mildly sarcastic expression. Dress it in a dark-green work vest and overalls, with small rounded hooves for hands. [Art style] Detailed painted storybook illustration: delicate gouache strokes, soft lighting, warm colors, polished finish. [Composition] Full body, standing front-facing and centered, with clear space around the character. [Background] Pure white #FFFFFF only—no cream, beige, paper texture, or warm tint. Keep all four corners white. No furniture, floor, or shadows. [Requirements] No watermark, corner badge, logo, or platform mark. Use bright clothing, not white or pale colors. Do not include text in the image.

Step Two:
Write the four-panel story.

Once the character is set, give it afour-panel story. One action and one line per panel. Because the GIF loops, the story should connect back to its first panel.

1 · SetupWhat everyday situation is the character in?
Example: slumped at a desk, scrolling on a phone
2 · TriggerWhat happens that gets a reaction?
Example: sees a requirements document and rolls its eyes
3 · PunchlineAdd a reversal or comic beat
Example: a meeting invite pops up; the character gives a crooked grin
4 · LoopConnect the last panel back to the first
Example: checks a payslip, then slumps back into the opening pose

Paste in the character description from step one, then add the four-panel story:

Continue with the same overworked office-worker mascot from the previous step: storybook style, small cow horns, white horse ears, brown mane, tiny black eyes, and dark-green work vest. Create a four-panel animated story. [Art style] Detailed painted storybook illustration, delicate strokes, soft lighting, warm colors. [Background] Pure white #FFFFFF in every image—no cream, beige, paper texture, or warm tint. Keep all corners white. No furniture, floor, or shadows. [Story] Panel 1 (setup): A mother cow surrounded by calves, looking warm and content. Caption: “Cow Herd.” Panel 2 (trigger): A horse surrounded by foals, happily frolicking. Caption: “Horse Herd.” Panel 3 (punchline): Cut to the mascot alone with a phone, surrounded by work-chat bubbles and no friends nearby; it looks utterly defeated. Caption: “Work Chat.” Panel 4 (loop): The mascot stares at the phone and reluctantly replies “Got it!” in the work chat. Caption: “Got It!” [Requirements] No watermark, badges, logos, or platform marks. White background in all four corners. Keep each caption under six characters where possible; the action should still be clear without captions. Panels 1 and 2 show real cows and horses with their own herds. Panels 3 and 4 show the overworked office-worker mascot, who only has a work chat and can only reply “Got it!” That contrast is the joke.

Step Three:
Ask AI to assemble the GIF.

Step two produced a four-panel image with captions. For step three, send this prompt to the AI and ask it touse a code toolto assemble the images into a looping GIF.

Combine the four sticker images from the previous step, in order, into a looping GIF using code. Do not just describe how to do it. Requirements: 1. Use panels in order 1 → 2 → 3 → 4. 2. Resize each panel to WeChat sticker dimensions, 240 × 240 pixels. 3. Loop continuously: show each panel for 0.5 seconds, hold the last panel for 1.5 seconds, then return to the first to create a clear ending. 4. Keep the file at or below 500 KB.

The AI can use code to check the white backgrounds, assemble the frames in order, and export the GIF. You just need to copy, review, and send it on WeChat.

Are all four captions legible and spelled correctly? Regenerate any panel with missing strokes or typos.
Check 2

Practice after the lesson:
Try another character and story.

Change the character from step one and the story from step two, then repeat the same three steps. Try one of these directions:

Change the characterCat, dog, panda, or an overworked rabbit
Round, chubby, and expressive animals make memorable office-worker mascots.
Change the storyOvertime · meetings · Mondays
“Tomorrow’s Friday,” “Left on read,” and “They changed the requirements again” are ready-made joke ideas.
Change the captionsKeep each caption to 2–6 characters
Use the same character and poses with new captions to create another sticker set. That’s the key to making variations in batches.
